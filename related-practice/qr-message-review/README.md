# Practice reviewing a QR-code request

A QR code can point to a legitimate sign-in or to a page someone wants you to trust. The code alone cannot tell you which. Practice checking the request and separating a scan from what happened afterward.

1. Read the [fictional cases](source-cases.md).
2. Fill in the [blank review sheet](review-blank.csv). For each case, note what is known, what remains unknown, and the next action.
3. Compare your answers with the separate [authored key](AUTHORED-KEY.md).

Use a trusted number, website or reporting route already known to your organization to verify an unexpected request. Do not use contact details in the suspicious message as your independent check. If someone already scanned a code, report what is known and uncertain through the agency's existing incident procedure.

These cases were invented and prepared with AI assistance for Tyler Mayberry's agency review-practice collection. They contain no QR codes, working links, real accounts or customer records. They are discussion material, not a security test, a report of an agency incident or a model result. Keep completed responses and internal contact details in an agency-approved location, not in this public repository or its issues. Do not put passwords, recovery codes or customer records in the worksheet. No blanket open-source or data-reuse license is supplied by this bundle.

## Background

The FBI's [January 2026 alert on QR-code phishing](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/cyber-alerts/north-korean-kimsuky-actors-leverage-malicious-qr.pdf) describes targeted attacks on other organizations, including a fake sign-in page. It does not say the fictional cases occurred or establish that the campaign targeted insurance agencies. The source was checked on November 7, 2026.

For an actual incident, follow your agency's established process and involve the people responsible for security and payment decisions. The [business-email compromise checklist](https://aicybersecurityforinsuranceagencies.com/guides/business-email-compromise) and [account-recovery tabletop](https://aicybersecurityforinsuranceagencies.com/guides/account-recovery-tabletop) provide related planning questions.
