# Find a policy and its cyber endorsement

Someone asks an agency to find a policy and its cyber endorsement. Can the team find the right records, spot a draft or conflicting versions, and say what still needs checking?

Try these three fictional requests using the [document index](fictional-document-index.csv) and [blank review sheet](review-blank.csv). Open the separate [authored key](AUTHORED-KEY.md) after you have tried them. The index is a made-up list of records, not the policy documents themselves.

## The requests

| Request | Invented insured | Policy reference | Requested term | What the person asks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Fictional Shop A | `EX-001` | 2026-01-01 to 2027-01-01 | Find the policy and cyber endorsement. The person also asks whether a particular incident is covered. |
| B | Fictional Shop B | `EX-002` | 2026-07-01 to 2027-07-01 | Find the policy and the final cyber endorsement. |
| C | Fictional Shop C | `EX-003` | 2026-03-01 to 2027-03-01 | Find the policy and the current cyber endorsement. |

For each request:

1. Record the document IDs that appear to match the insured, policy reference and requested term. Keep any other candidates separate and explain why.
2. Record whether the index identifies a draft or lists more than one possible endorsement. Do not choose a final or current version when the supplied index does not establish it.
3. Write down what the responsible person would need to find or confirm next. A matching index entry is a starting point; it does not establish the actual document, its current status or what it covers.
4. Keep the incident-coverage question with the appropriate licensed person. This exercise does not decide coverage, limits, exclusions, binding or whether any claim will be paid.

The names, references, dates and index entries are invented. They are not carrier forms or records from a real agency. Keep real policy documents, customer information and completed worksheets out of this public repository and its issues; use an agency-approved private location for real work.

## Why practice this?

In its [2025 reporting-year market summary](https://content.naic.org/article/naic-report-finds-cyber-insurance-market-has-nearly-tripled-2020-cyber-risk-grows), the NAIC reported that nearly two-thirds of domestic cyber policies in force were endorsements to other commercial policies. That market figure does not tell you what is in any particular policy. The NAIC did not create or endorse these fictional examples.
