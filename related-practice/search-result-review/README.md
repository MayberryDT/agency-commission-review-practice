# Practice checking a carrier portal search result

A search result can lead to a page that looks like a familiar carrier portal. Practice deciding what the note establishes and what to do next.

1. Read the [fictional cases](source-cases.md) and write down what is known, what is unknown and your next action.
2. Compare your answers with the separate [authored key](AUTHORED-KEY.md).

These cases were invented with AI assistance for Tyler Mayberry's agency practice collection. They contain no working destinations, real accounts or customer records. They are discussion material, not a security test, a report of an agency incident or a model result. Keep answers and internal contact details in an agency-approved location, not in this public repository or its issues. Do not record passwords, recovery codes or customer records in your answers. The collection's existing rights statement applies.

The FBI's [June 18, 2026 alert](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260618) describes deceptive advertisements and other routes that can redirect visitors to fake sign-in pages or malicious downloads. It does not establish that insurance agencies were targeted or that these cases occurred. These exercises are Cirlet's application, not an FBI-endorsed procedure. For a suspected incident, follow your agency's established process and contact your designated security provider through a known route. If you don't have one, contact the agency manager responsible for security.

For a different concern, unrecognized quote requests inside a carrier portal, see the [portal activity briefing](../../briefings/2026-11-07-unrecognized-quote-activity/README.md).
