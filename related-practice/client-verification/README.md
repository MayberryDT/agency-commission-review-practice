# Client wording for checking payment-change messages

An agency can adapt these sample messages to explain how a client should check a message claiming that payment instructions have changed. They are drafts for owner review, not an established agency policy or a live fraud test. No real client information is included.

## Files

- [Email wording](email.txt): a short paragraph for an appropriate existing client communication.
- [Website wording](website.txt): a public explanation with space for an established contact route.
- [Owner checks](owner-checks.txt): confirm the wording fits your actual process before use.

Replace every bracketed placeholder and have the responsible agency owner approve the result. Use a contact route the client can establish independently of the suspicious message. Do not invent a callback service, an approval requirement or a monitoring capability your agency does not provide. Coordinate payment questions with the relevant carrier or finance provider where needed.

Do not publish a staff member’s private details or paste client records into this repository. These files do not collect, send or verify anything. A callback or a familiar voice alone does not prove a request is legitimate.

## Source and limits

The FTC’s [How To Avoid a Scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scam), dated July 2023 and read October 24, 2026, recommends contacting a business through a trustworthy independently obtained route rather than the message’s links, supplied number or caller ID. The templates apply that general principle to a sample agency communication. They are not FTC-approved wording, an insurance rule or evidence of a current attack.
