# Authored answer guide

Open this after you discuss the three fictional cases. Other careful answers may identify useful questions. A known contact route helps verify a request; it does not guarantee that a person, device or support session is safe.

## Case 1: the request

The employee received an unexpected call and heard a claim that the caller works for the IT provider. The display name, claimed urgency and caller-supplied number do not establish identity or authorization. The case does not establish that the caller is an attacker.

Do not share the screen on that request yet. Verify the caller and the requested work using the agency's previously established contact route, not the number supplied by the caller. If that route does not answer, follow an already documented alternate escalation path or leave the request pending. An upcoming meeting is not evidence of authorization. The agency's designated IT or security contact should handle verification; the identity and reason remain unknown until checked.

## Case 2: a confirmed request with a limit

The agency's previously established contact route confirmed the appointment and caller. The stated procedure permits viewing a blank test window for that purpose. That confirmation is evidence about the specific request and stated scope, not blanket permission to expose the whole desktop or grant remote control.

Hold the expanded request. The employee should check it with the responsible agency owner and IT provider through an independently known route, and follow the agency's approval procedure before changing the scope. If the original limited step is approved, check what the selected window will show before sharing. A screen view and permission to control the computer are different questions; do not infer one from the other. Verification does not guarantee that the session is safe.

## Case 3: screen sharing already happened

The employee reports that they shared a screen for about 30 seconds and then ended sharing. They did not knowingly grant remote control. The caller's identity, what was visible, whether anything was recorded, and whether remote control was available or used remain unknown. A shared view alone neither proves a breach nor proves that remote control was impossible.

Tell the agency owner and the responsible IT or security contact promptly through an independently known route and follow the existing incident procedure. Report the approximate time, the request, the action taken and what the employee remembers or does not remember. Do not put passwords, one-time codes or client records in public GitHub issues. Follow the provider's instructions for securing and sharing any needed sensitive evidence through an approved private channel. The responsible people can decide what evidence to preserve, what to investigate and what response is appropriate. This exercise does not prescribe a technical remediation or establish an outcome.
